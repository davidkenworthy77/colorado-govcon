Two-step slide. Step one: the models went looking for an official source 1,115 times. Demand for the authoritative source is real, measured and enormous.

Step two, the twist, from Destination DC's brand-mode perception themes: "Promotional Bias" and "Cross-Check Details". The models find the DMO site and then tell the traveler to double-check it.

Say: being official gets you retrieved. Being verifiable gets you believed. Awards win the shortlist (28.7% of category questions hunt for proof) and fade once you're named. What holds up under investigation: fact sheets, FAQs written as real questions, dated pages, the same facts on your site, Google Business Profile and Wikipedia, and showing your sources.

Taste

Every answer is written for one person.

Two travelers ask the same question and get different answers. There is no rank to hold.

3.6%

of the model's research seeks opinion. Almost nobody publishes it well.

What survives synthesis is a point of view, with a person behind it.

MMGY destination study, 2026; Beverly Hills scan, Jul 2026.

Every answer is assembled for one person. Two travelers ask the same question and get different answers. There's no rank to hold and no position to defend.

What survives synthesis is perspective. why-whistler and history-of-west-hollywood are both in the top-cited pages. Only 3.6% of the model's research seeks opinion, so almost nobody publishes it well, and it's the one thing a model can't manufacture.

Say: personalization is taste at the individual level. A named local who's actually eaten there beats "best restaurants in Breckenridge" every time. Trusted content has a point of view and a person behind it.
